The transition from ballistic to diffusive regimes in 3D subsonic turbulence is governed by the "Lagrangian memory" of tracers trapped in transient, small-scale vortex filaments. While previous results show Gaussian statistics at the macroscopic level, the hypothesis is that the **velocity autocorrelation function (VACF) exhibits a multi-scale decay** where the short-time ballistic regime is dominated by large-scale solenoidal forcing, but the intermediate-time "crossover" is modulated by the **local vorticity-tracer correlation**. Specifically, I propose that the "sticky" vortex events are not merely random, but are spatially clustered in regions of high strain-rate magnitude. By computing the **Conditional Lagrangian Velocity Autocorrelation (CL-VACF)**—conditioned on the local Q-criterion value at the tracer's position—we will demonstrate that tracers in high-Q regions exhibit a significantly longer memory (slower VACF decay) than those in high-strain regions. This will reveal that the observed Gaussianity is an ensemble average of two distinct sub-populations: a "trapped" population with persistent memory and a "ballistic/diffusive" population in the turbulent background, providing a microscopic explanation for the rapid convergence to Brownian motion in 3D.
